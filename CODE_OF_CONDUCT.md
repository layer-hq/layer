# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume good faith. Criticize code, not the person who
wrote it.

## Expected behavior

Everyone who takes part in this project — issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions — is expected to:

- treat other participants with respect, regardless of experience, background,
  identity, or how strongly they disagree with you;
- keep feedback specific and about the work: what is wrong, why it matters, what
  would be better;
- accept that maintainers may decline a change, and that "no" to a patch is not
  a judgment of the person who sent it;
- respect people's stated names and pronouns;
- take responsibility when you get something wrong, apologize, and move on.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Insults, personal attacks, or demeaning comments — including ones dressed up
  as technical criticism.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Posting other people's screenshots, screen recordings, or captured screen
  content in issues or pull requests without their consent. Layer captures
  screens; be careful what you attach, and never attach someone else's.
- Encouraging or defending any of the above.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — the repository, issues, pull requests,
reviews, discussions, and releases — and to conduct elsewhere when someone is
representing the project or when off-project behavior makes participation here
unsafe for someone else.

## Reporting

To report behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, use GitHub's private
reporting on the repository's **Security** tab (**Report a vulnerability**). It
is the project's private channel to the maintainers; a conduct report sent
through it is read the same way and is not treated as a security issue. Say up
front that it is a conduct report.

Include what happened, where, when, and links if you have them. You do not need
to prove anything or arrive with a conclusion — a description is enough.

Reports are handled confidentially. The maintainers will not share your identity
with the person reported without asking you first. If a report concerns a
maintainer, it will be handled by the other maintainers, who will recuse the
person involved.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying these standards and for responding to
reports. Depending on severity and pattern, a response may be:

1. **A private word** — an explanation of what was wrong and why.
2. **A warning** — with a defined period of no interaction with the people
   involved. Breaking it escalates.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban**, for sustained or severe violations.

Maintainers may also edit, hide, or delete comments, commits, code, and issues
that violate this Code of Conduct, and will say when they have done so.

Enforcement decisions are made in good faith and are not up for public
relitigation, though anyone subject to one may appeal privately through the same
reporting channel.
